How to read this
Hit chance (the big %)
How often this pick should win. We take every sportsbook's price, remove the cut each book keeps for itself (called the "vig"), and average them. 60% means it should win about 6 times in 10.
The line
"Over 65.5 rushing yards" wins at 66 or more. The .5 means there are no ties. "Anytime TD" wins if the player scores at least one touchdown, running or catching.
The odds
−115 means you bet $115 to win $100. +140 means you bet $100 to win $140. On Kalshi, a 43¢ YES pays $1 if it happens, so you pay 43¢ for a $1 ticket.
Best bets (the default order)
Picks with at least a 40% hit chance, scored on value (the price vs. the true odds, after a safety margin) and, just as much, on how likely they are to hit, with nudges for what the stats say and for players with real roles. On "Everything" the list takes turns across prop types (TDs, rushing, receiving, catches, passing, game lines), so the first screen shows the best of each instead of 40 of one kind. "No TD" bets and anything priced shorter than −400 are hidden.
Search and player pages
Type a player's name in the search bar to see only his picks (a team name works too). Tap a player's name for every line every book has on him, each side's hit chance and best price, Kalshi's prices, his hit-rate chart and the stats projection.
Why most sportsbook picks sit near 50%
Books set their main line (like Over/Under 65.5) so both sides are about even. Higher hit chances come from lower lines: "alternate lines" at a book (e.g. Over 39.5 at −300) or Kalshi's lower thresholds (e.g. 40+). They win more often but pay much less.
Value (the line under the meter)
Compares what you get paid with the hit chance. "Good value" means the book pays more than the pick is worth. Most picks are a bit below fair because every book keeps a cut. A likely pick can still be a bad deal.
Why this pick (stats)
Each pick has a one-line "Why" and a panel with usage, efficiency, matchup and game-context numbers from nflverse. A simple stats projection (usage × opponent-adjusted efficiency × game script) is compared with the line: Stats agree raises the confidence tag, Stats disagree lowers it and shows a warning. The hit chance itself always stays the sportsbooks' number. Tap any dotted stat name for a plain-English definition.
Confidence tag
Medium when 3+ books price the pick (Low with fewer). One step up if stats agree, one step down if they disagree, and one step down for a serious warning (player injury listing, strong wind on a passing pick, a line move against you).
Injuries (live)
Injury statuses come live from ESPN's injury feed, with the time and latest note. When a teammate is Out or Doubtful, his share of the team's targets or carries is handed to the players still active, in proportion to the shares they already have, and the stats projection moves with it. If injury news came out after the sportsbook lines were pulled, the pick says so: books have probably moved that line, so check your book before betting.
"Big gap: check injury news"
When one price is far from all the others, the usual reason is news (an injury, a benched player) that our last scan doesn't know about. Check before betting.
Custom picks and changing a line
Tap "Try another line" on any pick, or "+ Custom pick", to price any player or game at any line (say Over 52.5 instead of 65.5). In the slip, use − and + on a leg to move its line. The hit chance comes from the same sportsbook consensus as the list, moved to your line with the same model. Type your book's odds to see whether it's good value.
Over / Under filter
Show only overs (over a line, YES on Kalshi, scores a TD) or only unders. Moneylines and spreads have no over or under, so they're hidden while the filter is on.
Bankroll and stakes
Set a bankroll (money only for betting) and value picks show a suggested stake: a fraction of the Kelly formula, capped per bet. Picks without an edge say "No bet". Small, steady stakes are what survive losing streaks.
My bets and Track record
Save bets from the slip; finished games are graded from final stats, with your record, profit and return. Track record shows how the logged picks have actually done, so you can see whether "Stats agree" picks hit more.
Parlays
Every leg must win. The chances multiply, so three 60% legs hit together only about 22% of the time. Tap "+ Parlay" on a pick to add it to your slip at the bottom.
What this is not
It isn't a prediction model. It's math on the books' own prices from the last scan. It won't see late news, weather or lineup changes.

    Picks priced shorter than −400 (about 80¢ on Kalshi) and "No TD" bets are hidden: they pay too little to matter. Sportsbook data from The Odds API.
