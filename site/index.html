How to read this
Hit chance (the big %)
How often this pick should win. We take every sportsbook's price, remove the cut each book keeps for itself (called the "vig"), and average them. 60% means it should win about 6 times in 10.
The line
"Over 65.5 rushing yards" wins at 66 or more. The .5 means there are no ties. "Anytime TD" wins if the player scores at least one touchdown, running or catching.
The odds
−115 means you bet $115 to win $100. +140 means you bet $100 to win $140. On Kalshi, a 43¢ YES pays $1 if it happens, so you pay 43¢ for a $1 ticket.
Best bets (the default order)
Picks with at least a 40% hit chance, ranked by value (how the price compares with the true odds, after a safety margin), nudged up when they're more likely to hit and when the stats agree, and down when the stats disagree. "No TD" bets and anything priced shorter than −400 are hidden everywhere: they almost always win but pay almost nothing. Each player shows once per prop type, at the best line for the chosen order.
Why most sportsbook picks sit near 50%
Books set their main line (like Over/Under 65.5) so both sides are about even. Higher hit chances come from lower lines: "alternate lines" at a book (e.g. Over 39.5 at −300) or Kalshi's lower thresholds (e.g. 40+). They win more often but pay much less.
Value (the line under the meter)
Compares what you get paid with the hit chance. "Good value" means the book pays more than the pick is worth. Most picks are a bit below fair because every book keeps a cut. A likely pick can still be a bad deal.
Why this pick (stats)
Each pick has a one-line "Why" and a panel with usage, efficiency, matchup and game-context numbers from nflverse. A simple stats projection (usage × opponent-adjusted efficiency × game script) is compared with the line: Stats agree raises the confidence tag, Stats disagree lowers it and shows a warning. The hit chance itself always stays the sportsbooks' number. Tap any dotted stat name for a plain-English definition.
Confidence tag
Medium when 3+ books price the pick (Low with fewer). One step up if stats agree, one step down if they disagree, and one step down for a serious warning (player injury listing, strong wind on a passing pick, a line move against you).
"Big gap: check injury news"
When one price is far from all the others, the usual reason is news (an injury, a benched player) that our last scan doesn't know about. Check before betting.
Parlays
Every leg must win. The chances multiply, so three 60% legs hit together only about 22% of the time. Tap "+ Parlay" on a pick to add it to your slip at the bottom.
What this is not
It isn't a prediction model. It's math on the books' own prices from the last scan. It won't see late news, weather or lineup changes.

    Picks priced shorter than −400 (about 80¢ on Kalshi) and "No TD" bets are hidden: they pay too little to matter. Sportsbook data from The Odds API. Kalshi prices refresh every 30 seconds while you're on the Kalshi view. Most bettors lose money over time. Only bet what you can afford to lose.
